Digital Literacy

  • Overview
  • Assessments
  • Syllabus

This course introduces students to the core principles of digital literacy, focusing on the effective use of digital technologies for learning, communication, and information management. It emphasizes the development of essential digital skills needed to navigate, evaluate, and use online information responsibly in academic and professional contexts.

Students are also exposed to practical applications of digital tools, including the use of online platforms and development environments to support productivity, collaboration, and problem-solving. The course highlights the importance of ethical and responsible use of digital resources in a connected environment.

Key Learning Areas
  • Fundamentals of digital literacy and online competencies
  • Effective information searching, evaluation, and usage
  • Responsible and ethical use of digital technologies
  • Digital communication and collaboration tools
  • Introduction to productivity and learning platforms
Digital Skills Communication Skills

This course assessment evaluates students through a combination of individual and group work. Each component contributes to the final grade, focusing on continuous learning, practical application, teamwork, and final evaluation.

Continuous Assessment (100%)

Evaluates ongoing performance throughout the semester including participation, quizzes, in-class activities, assignments, and presentations. This assessment includes both individual and group tasks.

1. Individual Assessment (10%)
  • This assignment requires students to identify, read, and analyze one primary research article related to Digital Literacy, assessing its relevance, strengths, and limitations.
  • This task requires students to summarize, analyze, and critique a primary research article related to Digital Literacy.
2. Group Assessment (20%)
  • Each group will consist of four (4) membersand will research a topic related to Digital Literacy. The findings must be presented in an instructional videowith a minimum length of three (3) minutes.
  • The primary objectives are to communicate clear and accurate information in an engaging and accessible manner for an academic audience.
3. Final Class Project (40%)
  • This final project is designed to provide students with hands-on experience in planning, organizing, and executing an informational eventrelated to Digital Literacy. The project allows students to conduct and manage either a Webinar, Professional Talk, Sharing Session, or similar eventaimed at disseminating knowledge, insights, latest trends, and practical tips for individual or societal development in the field of digital literacy.
4. Final Test (30%)
  • Written test assessing overall understanding of the course.
Course Syllabus

This syllabus introduces the core concepts of digital literacy, beginning with fundamental digital skills and online safety, and progressing toward effective information evaluation, responsible technology use, and the application of digital tools in academic and professional environments.

1. Introduction
  • Models and theories
  • Concepts
  • Evolution
  • Definition
  • Principles
2. Information Skills
  • Classification (seven levels)
  • Theory and practice
  • Elements of Information Skills
  • Importance of Information Skills
3. Digital Literacy Tools
  • World wide web
  • Cloud computing
  • Web apps
  • Social media
  • Learning Management Systems (LMS)
  • Mobile apps
  • Other tools
4. Governing Policies
  • Privacy
  • Piracy
  • Net Neutrality
  • Data Protection
  • Digital Trust
5. Digital Citizenship
  • Overview
  • 10 Elements of Digital Citizenship
6. Digital Divine
  • Definition
  • Economic Divide
  • Usability Divide
  • Empowerement Divide
  • Bridging Solution
7. Issues in Digital Literacy
  • Authentication information
  • Cyber security
  • Excessive internet used
  • Others
